## How to use and maintain this list

1. Find the closest existing approach ID before starting work. Read its result, root-cause confidence, and reopening condition.
2. For a new attempt, record the parent ID, the specific mechanism being changed, why that change addresses the recorded failure, the smallest disconfirming test, and the stop rule **before** running it. If none of those changed, do not repeat the experiment.
3. Preserve failed examples and original results. Do not change answer keys, exclude inconvenient fonts, or call previously inspected data an unseen holdout.
4. At closeout, record the dataset, denominators, false approvals, bold detection, Review count where available, failures/timeouts, timing scope, evidence location, and decision. Separate observed failures from inferred causes.
5. Update this list in the same commit as new experimental evidence when possible. Append a dated decision-history entry. Supersede old conclusions explicitly; do not erase them.
6. Mark **RESOLVED** only for the specific problem actually verified. A fixed locator, passing unit tests, or successful batch accounting does not resolve boldness classification or release readiness.

**Statuses:** `FAILED` = measured implementation missed its gate; `PARTIAL` = a subproblem improved but the requirement remains incomplete; `OPEN` = diagnosed, not fixed; `UNTESTED` = hypothesis only; `EXCLUDED` = outside current scope or constraints; `RESOLVED` = bounded fix verified with evidence. “Root cause unknown” is an acceptable finding.

## R-037 preregistration: heading-versus-body relative stroke weight at deployed sizes (diagnostic)

**Status before execution: PROPOSED, not run.** Parent R-010 and blocker D-001; related to the D-001 invariant preserved in `docs/plans/requirement-3-tdd.md`. Registered September 24, 2026 by the investigation handover; see [decision memo](docs/boldness-decision-memo-2026-09-24.md).

Hypothesis and changed mechanism. Every attempt from R-009 to R-036 classifies the *absolute* weight of a heading crop against a *source-weight* label (usWeightClass 400/700). The R-016 contact sheet shows Superclarendon 400 with stems as thick as Georgia 700 and TI-Nspire 700; Datatype 400/700 share identical outlines; Alfa Slab One's only style is named Regular. A heading-only crop therefore does not contain the information that the label asks for; the label is the designer's naming, not a visual property. The assignment's reviewer checks whether the heading is bold *within the printed warning statement*, and the health-warning regulation (27 CFR 16.22, recalled from memory; ecfr.gov is unreachable from this environment and must be verified) requires the heading in bold type and the remainder not in bold type. Three things change relative to R-010: (1) the target is heading weight relative to the same statement's body, so a bold body is a correctly non-matching (non-compliant) statement rather than a required positive, withdrawing the D-001 invariant; (2) the measurement is a per-pixel local thickness, the minimum of the horizontal and vertical ink run through each ink pixel, summarized by the median over the region and normalized by that region's measured cap height, with no skeletonization; (3) the operating range is cap height ≥16 px, the floor the deployed guard already enforces, so R-010's cap-9/12/14 measurements are outside scope rather than silently dropped.

Why this is not a repeat. R-010 scored bold-body positives as required matches and measured skeleton thickness at cap heights of 9–14 px; its recorded counterexample (PT Mono cap-14 regular 1.121 versus TI-Nspire cap-12 bold 1.045) sits below the deployed 16 px floor.
