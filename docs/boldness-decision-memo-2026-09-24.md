# Decision memo: automated warning-boldness detection

Date: September 24, 2026. Scope: requirement 3 of [ASSIGNMENT.md](../ASSIGNMENT.md) (the `GOVERNMENT WARNING:` heading must be uppercase and bold) and its interaction with requirement 5 (results in about five seconds). Canonical ledger: [REQUIREMENTS.md attempt history](../REQUIREMENTS.md#attempt-history). Everything below was checked against code and artifacts on `main` at `1f1d943`; no newer commits existed on `origin/main` at the start of this review.

## 1. Diagnosis

The boldness detector keeps failing because every attempt from R-009 through R-036 tries to recover a **source-weight label** (the font file's usWeightClass, 400 versus 700) from a **heading-only crop**. That label is not a visual property of the crop. A heavy "Regular" face and a bold face of a lighter family have the same stems; nothing inside a two-word crop says which one the designer named "Bold". The evidence is direct:

- The R-016 contact sheet ([evidence/render-diagnostic-r016-corrected/contact.png](../evidence/render-diagnostic-r016-corrected/contact.png)) shows Superclarendon 400 with stems as thick as Georgia 700 and TI-Nspire 700. Both rendering paths, at 11 px and 24 px, approved it (4/4 false approvals, 0 flips).
- R-035's input gate found 32 Datatype 400/700 pairs with byte-identical warning glyph pixels and opposite labels ([evidence/r035-label-conflicts.json](../evidence/r035-label-conflicts.json)). They were quarantined as a data defect. They are better read as a demonstration that the label is metadata.
- Alfa Slab One's only style is named Regular (weight 400). In R-038 its heading has an absolute stroke score of 0.40–0.45, above every 700-weight heading of 14 families ([evidence/r038-result.json](../evidence/r038-result.json), `evaluation.absolute`).
- After training on 22,144 images including Superclarendon itself, R-036 still scores the JPEG-75 Superclarendon regular at 0.99962 while its PNG siblings score below 0.08 ([evidence/r036-result.json](../evidence/r036-result.json)). The head memorised pixels of the trained variants; it did not learn a weight concept, because there is none to learn from the crop alone.

What the assignment actually asks for is a reviewer's judgement: is the heading printed bold **within the warning statement on this label**. The health-warning regulation, as I recall it (27 CFR 16.22(a), "The words 'GOVERNMENT WARNING' must appear in capital letters and in bold type. The remainder of the warning statement may not appear in bold type."), defines the same relation: heading bold, remainder not bold. I could not verify the wording from this environment (ecfr.gov, law.cornell.edu, govinfo.gov and ttb.gov are blocked by the egress proxy); the repository contains no copy. Verify it before relying on the exact words. Either way the operational reading is the same: boldness is a **contrast between the heading and the rest of the statement**, and the remainder is part of the evidence.

This reframing was available from the start and was rejected once. R-010 measured heading-versus-body stroke thickness and was closed as FAILED, and blocker D-001 was written as "relative contrast is not heading-only weight", with a required invariant that identical heading pixels keep their verdict when the body becomes bold. That invariant is the opposite of the regulation: a statement whose remainder is bold is non-compliant, and refusing to match it is the correct product outcome. R-010's other defects were measurement quality (skeleton thickness at 9–14 px cap heights, below the 16 px floor the deployed guard already enforces) and its per-image evidence never being published.

Two bounded diagnostics this session tested the reframing with hash-pinned OFL fonts and preregistered criteria. R-037 (integer run-length estimator) failed and identified two specific causes: integer quantization (688/688 thickness values were integers) and capital stems being heavier than lowercase stems within a family. R-038 corrected both on the same frozen images: a sub-pixel estimator with a **capitals-only body reference** separated 700 from 400 headings with zero overlap across 14 families, retained 16/16 bold headings per family at the gap midpoint, and rejected both compliance controls (Alfa Slab One over itself at most 1.10; bold body at most 1.07; bold minimum 1.13). With the reference restricted to body capitals of at least 16 px the gap is 10% (1.195 versus 1.084). Absolute scoring on the same pixels overlapped on 32 rows.

**Certainty.** High that the absolute source-weight target is unrecoverable from a heading crop for heavy-regular faces, and therefore that further training, more families, better optimisation or a different backbone cannot pass the zero-false-approval gate on that target. Moderate that the relative capitals-only measurement carries through OCR localisation and real artwork: R-038 is synthetic, perfect-location, black-on-white, 14 exposed families, and its full-set margin is thin where body capitals are under 16 px. The five-second failure is separate and fully explained: the cloud round trip alone takes 4.5 s ([evidence/pile-ui-production.json](../evidence/pile-ui-production.json)); a local measurement removes it.

## 3. Mistakes in problem definition, evaluation and process

1. **Target definition.** "Bold" was never written down. The fixtures operationalised it as usWeightClass 700 versus 400 of a heading crop. That is neither the reviewer's judgement nor the regulation's relation. The R-035 eligibility rule (families must have both 400 and 700) even excluded the single-weight display faces that make the problem obvious.
2. **The D-001 invariant** required the classifier to approve a bold heading over a bold body. That contradicts the remainder-not-bold rule and removed the only in-image reference that makes the task well-posed.
3. **Fixture sizes.** Cap heights of 9–14 px dominate the recorded misses and the R-010 counterexample, yet the deployed guard rejects anything under 16 px. Failures below the operating floor were counted against candidates instead of being declared out of scope.
4. **Gate applied to an ill-posed target.** The zero-observed-false-approval and 95%-recall criterion is reasonable as an engineering gate for a well-posed target. Applied to source weight it guaranteed failure, and three 80-image qualification sets were burned discovering that. Note also that zero errors in 40 negatives bounds the false-approval rate only to roughly 7% at 95% confidence; the gate should be kept, but stated as a screen, not a proof.
5. **Experiment loop.** Thirty-six numbered attempts across evidence dated September 22–24 (the git history holds 51 commits on September 23–24), each varying one training or preprocessing knob on the same paradigm. Closeouts proposed the next knob instead of asking whether the target was recoverable. R-017's "heavy regular maps near bold training features" was the answer and was read as a coverage gap.
6. **Reproducibility.** The core rendering and packing code, the FontDNA ONNX (Hugging Face is blocked here), proprietary macOS fonts and all model checkpoints live outside the repository. Several recorded failures cannot be re-audited from a clean clone. R-010's rejection rests on a report whose per-image file is absent.
7. **Latency accounting.** The five-second requirement was treated as a tuning problem while the architecture spent 4–4.5 s on a network round trip with a 4 s deadline. A prototype for a firewalled agency (Marcus's note) should not need an outbound ML call at all.
8. **Documentation weight.** REQUIREMENTS.md is 97 KB and the handoff repeats its content. Useful for audit, but the "read everything before acting" rule has become a cost that the next engineer pays with no index of which conclusions are load-bearing. This memo is intended to be that index.

## 4. Paths compared and the recommendation

**Path A (recommended): local heading-versus-statement weight contrast.** Measure sub-pixel stroke thickness of the OCR-located heading and of the capital letters in the OCR-located warning body; match only when the heading is materially heavier. Addresses both observed failures: heavy regular faces (ratio ≈1 against their own body), and misses at small sizes (no learned size-invariance; the guard's 16 px floor applies to both regions). Earlier experiments do not reject it: R-010 is the only relative attempt, its criterion was wrong (D-001), its estimator was skeleton thickness at 9–14 px, and its evidence is unpublished. Supporting evidence: R-010's own renderer table (bold/regular ratios 1.35–1.63 for five families, including Superclarendon 1.41), and R-038. Effort: one JavaScript module (~150 lines, the R-038 estimator is already vectorisable), body-capital localisation from Tesseract symbol boxes, cutoff freeze, removal of the cloud call from the critical path. Runtime: milliseconds after OCR; no network; batch cost unchanged. Main risk: OCR symbol boxes for four body capitals may be unreliable on real artwork, and the margin narrows for low-contrast bold families (Averia 1.13–1.20 at 16–20 px). Falsified if R-039 shows regular headings above bold headings at OCR-located ≥16 px regions, or if fewer than 95% of clear bold fixtures yield a usable capitals reference.

**Path B: keep the absolute classifier, add the body as a tie-breaker.** Run the R-036 head in-browser (the resize-parity helper was groundwork for this) and require both a high absolute score and a heading-heavier-than-body ratio. Addresses false approvals but not misses, keeps a 40 px-tall normalised input that already lost the small-size cases, needs the FontDNA ONNX and cached features that are not in the repository and cannot be fetched from here, and adds model download time to the first-use path. Not rejected by prior work, but strictly more complex than A with no evidence it adds recall. Falsified if Path A alone meets the gate.

**Path C: keep the cloud two-model consensus and fix latency.** Rejected: both models approved Superclarendon regular (R-005) and Century (R-002) independently of latency, the 4 s deadline is already the whole budget, and it violates the firewall constraint. No experiment suggests a prompt fixes a target the models cannot see either.

Path A is recommended because it is the only one whose target is recoverable from the image, it removes the latency and network blockers at the same time, it is the simplest, and it now has a passing preregistered development result. The paired renderer trace proposed in the previous handoff should not run: R-015/R-016 showed both renderers fail on Superclarendon at 24 px, so renderer parity cannot change the decision.

## 5. Smallest decisive next experiment: R-039 (proposed, not run)

Question: does the R-038 measurement survive real OCR localisation on full-label images at deployed sizes?

- **Inputs (fixed before scoring).** Full-label PNG/JPEG fixtures rendered with the same pinned OFL fonts as R-038 (14 exposed families; the eight reserved families stay closed), using the existing corpus layout style: brand, class, ABV, volume, producer, then the complete warning statement with heading in 400 or 700 and body in 400, heading cap heights 16, 20, 28 and 40 px at 1800 px longest side, plus the bold-body and Alfa Slab One controls. Record font and image hashes.
- **Pipeline.** The application's Tesseract.js worker with PSM 11 (as in `scripts/verify-appearance-guard.js`), the existing `headingBox` locator, body capitals from symbol boxes of words that match the required wording, the R-038 estimator ported to JavaScript with a parity test against the Python values on ten R-038 images.
- **Controls.** Perfect-location scores from R-038 for the same heading/body faces, to attribute any change to localisation.
- **Expected outcomes.** If Path A is right: every regular heading below 1.12, every bold heading with a usable reference above 1.19 at ≥16 px, and a usable reference on at least 95% of fixtures. If OCR degrades the reference, ratios scatter and the usable-reference rate drops.
- **Pass/fail.** Zero regular Match at the R-038 midpoint; ≥95% bold Match per family among fixtures with a usable reference; ≥95% usable-reference rate. Cutoff frozen from R-038 before scoring, not fitted to R-039.
- **Stop rule.** One run. A failure on localisation is fixed only in localisation with the measurement frozen; a failure on separation rejects Path A at deployed sizes and returns to Path B.
- **Budget.** Local Node, minutes. No cloud, no reserved families, no runtime change.

## 7. Unknowns and what would change the recommendation

- **Real artwork.** No independently annotated real labels exist (D-004). R-038 and R-039 are synthetic. Ten packet images with provisional agent observations remain unscored. The measurement's behaviour on photographed, curved or low-contrast labels is unknown; the guard will abstain on much of it, which is acceptable for the prototype but must be reported.
- **Body-capital localisation.** The reference depends on four capitals. If Tesseract symbol boxes on small body text prove unreliable in R-039, the fallback is the whole-body reference with a per-family unknown offset, which R-038 rejected. That result would push toward Path B.
- **Inverted polarity and colour.** R-038 used black on white. The deployed guard already assumes dark ink; canonicalisation (R-023's transform) can be applied before measurement, untested here.
- **Heavy-regular proprietary faces.** Superclarendon, Century, Hoefler and TI-Nspire could not be rendered in this environment. The R-005 v4 fixtures exist locally on the original machine and should be scored in R-039 as a regression set. A heading in a heavy face over a light body of a different family will be called bold by construction; that is a documented assumption, not an error.
- **Regulation wording.** If the actual text does not define boldness relative to the remainder, the relative measure is still the reviewer's operational judgement, but the "bold remainder is non-compliant" rejection would become a product choice to document rather than a rule to cite.
- **What would change the recommendation.** R-039 failing on separation at OCR-located ≥16 px regions; a usable-reference rate well under 95% on clear fixtures; or evidence that real labels commonly set the statement in a single heavy face, which the relative measure cannot approve and a human must review.
